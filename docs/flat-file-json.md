# Storage Strategy: Flat JSON Files (One File per Post)

The proposal: instead of (or partly instead of) a relational database, application data lives as plain JSON files. Every post is one file, and meetings, groups, RSVPs and memberships could be too. The files could live in **object storage** (R2, S3, Backblaze B2, Bunny, Tigris and others) or on **local disk** on a VPS. This fits the static-first baseline: the renderer reads the files to build the pre-rendered site, and browsers can fetch selected JSON directly from the CDN.

**Researched:** 2026-09-29. Seven topics, each researched from official docs and pricing pages and then re-checked by a second agent trying to refute it (12 to 18 corrections per topic), plus a critic pass that found gaps and contradictions between the topics. **Nothing was tested live**: every capability claim (conditional writes, consistency, limits) is from documentation, and the local timings were measured on a ZFS filesystem inside a container, not on the ext4 or XFS disk a VPS would have. Prices are list prices with no credits, and change often.

**Load model used** (from your inputs): 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 groups, each with 1 meeting a month, about 20 RSVPs per meeting, 40 members, and 10 / 440 / 1,500 messages per group per month (low / mid / high, from 5×2, about 28×16 and 50×30). One message is one post row of about 0.5 KB of JSON. 1% of messages carry a 1.5 MB attachment. Attachments are ordinary binary files, not JSON.

**Companion documents:** `cloudflare.md`, `aws.md`, `bunny.md`, `email.md`.

---

## Verdict

**Files are a good derived read model and a poor system of record.**

- **Public read side (works well):** pages and JSON for groups, meetings, threads and the directory, served from a CDN. Cargo's sparse index, CocoaPods and Homebrew all moved from git to static files for exactly this reason.
- **Write side (works badly on its own):** the PRD has invariants and queries that object stores were not built for: unique RSVPs, one owner, the 150-member cap, single-use magic links, rate limits, reminder claims, organizer analytics, and erasure of one person's data. Nothing surveyed runs a many-writer, invariant-heavy, personal-data app on one file per record with no coordinator (this is a survey result, not a proof).
- **Cost:** on request-priced stores, one PUT per post costs about 60 months of what it costs to keep the same 0.8 KB as a row on a $0.10-per-GB volume. At 10,000 groups, per-post files on R2 cost about $17 to $37 a month at the mid case and $65 to $140 at the high case (excluding attachments), against $0 marginal for rows in SQLite or Postgres on a server you already run.
- **The exception:** stores that charge nothing per request (Backblaze B2, Scaleway) make files cheap, but not every one supports the conditional writes that the invariants depend on.

**Recommended:** keep the system of record in a small transactional store (SQLite with Litestream, or Postgres), and use files as derived artifacts on the CDN. Use per-post or per-thread files as canonical content only if you have a specific reason, and then only with a coordinator.

## Three designs

| | Design 1: database first, files derived | Design 2: hybrid (immutable post files plus coordinator) | Design 3: pure files, no database |
|---|---|---|---|
| **System of record** | SQLite (WAL, one writer in the Go process) or Postgres holds every row, including post bodies | One immutable JSON file per post. A small SQLite index maps thread to post keys | Files only, with deterministic keys and compare-and-swap |
| **Files are** | Derived: one static page per group and meeting, a public JSON projection, directory tiles, regenerated by a debounced job | Canonical for posts, with nightly compaction of old months into per-group-month JSONL segments | Canonical for everything |
| **Coordinator** | The database itself | Small SQLite: RSVP uniqueness, roster and cap, owner, tokens, rate limits, reminder claims, quotas, reverse indexes, outbox | None. Roster file for the cap and owner, marker keys for jobs, in-process limiter |
| **Durability** | Litestream to R2 or B2 (10 s sync: about 355k PUTs a month, inside R2's free allowance, RPO 10 s) | Same, plus the post store | Store's own durability only |
| **Marginal data-tier cost** | $0 to about $5 a month, plus a volume ($0.07 to $49 a month across the scenarios) | Warm-writer at 10,000 groups high on R2: about $68 to $135 a month for post and page PUTs; on Scaleway or B2 about $19 to $77 | The full PUT bill; holes remain (see below) |
| **Where it breaks** | Single VPS writer (minutes of restore) | Two things to keep consistent | Exact rate limits, sub-day expiry, multi-key operations, analytics, point-in-time recovery, job retries |

**Where files are clearly worse than SQLite or Postgres:**
- Any per-person or cross-group query (erasure, "all RSVPs for this email" when an account verifies, analytics, admin lists).
- Exact counters and rate limits.
- Multi-key atomicity: ownership transfer, join plus cap plus preferences, delete a meeting only if it has no RSVPs.
- Point-in-time recovery and cross-key consistent backups.
- Object-count scale: above about 1 to 3 million files, default ext4 runs out of inodes, and backup and restore times reach hours at 55M+ files.
- Cost at high volume, unless the store has zero request fees.
- Schema migration: one PUT per object (about $2,500 at 562M objects on R2).

**Where files are fine or better:**
- The public read side on a CDN.
- Per-group export and deletion by prefix.
- Immutable attachments.
- A reminders due-index (a LIST of one prefix, about $0.04 a month).
- Directory tiles.

**Crossover.** At 100 to 1,000 groups in the mid case the request bill on R2 is about $10 to $38 a month, comparable to a $12 droplet. From 1,000 groups high or 10,000 groups mid, database rows or zero-request stores win.

## Object-store semantics

The invariants depend on **conditional writes**: create-if-absent (`If-None-Match: *`) for uniqueness, and compare-and-swap (`If-Match: <ETag>`) for the owner, the cap and single-use tokens. Documented support varies a lot.

| Store | Create-if-absent | Compare-and-swap | Conditional delete | Consistency | Write limit | Min size or retention | Versioning | PUT / GET per 1,000 | Storage $/GB-month | Egress |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amazon S3** | Yes (2024-08-20) | Yes (2024-11-25) | Yes (2025-09-16) | Strong, incl. LIST | 3,500 PUT and 5,500 GET per second per prefix | None (Standard) | Yes | $0.005 / $0.0004 | $0.023 | $0.09/GB |
| **Cloudflare R2** | Yes | Yes (atomicity under concurrent writers is undocumented) | **No** | Strong, incl. LIST | **1 write per second per key** | None | **Not supported** | $0.0045 / $0.00036 | $0.015 | Free |
| **Google Cloud Storage** | Yes, via generation match 0 (not `If-None-Match`) | Yes, via generation | Yes | Strong | 1 write per second per key | None (7-day soft delete by default) | Yes | $0.005 / $0.0004 | $0.020 | $0.12/GiB |
| **Azure Blob** | Yes (returns 409, not 412) | Yes | Yes | Strong | 3,000 requests per second per blob | None (hot) | Yes | $0.005 / $0.0004 | $0.0208 | $0.087/GB |
| **Tigris** | Yes | Yes | Unknown | Strong in single- and multi-region buckets (the global default is eventual) | None documented | None (Standard) | No bucket versioning | $0.005 / $0.0005 | $0.020 | Free |
| **Scaleway** | Yes | Yes | Unknown | Strong for PUT, DELETE and LIST | Limit exists, numbers unpublished | Not documented (billed by GB-hour) | Yes | **$0 / $0** | $0.0182 | 75 GB free, then €0.01/GB |
| Backblaze B2 | **No** (a 501 was reported in May 2025) | No | No | Not stated in the docs | 500 requests per second per account | None (old versions are billed) | Always versioned | **$0 / $0** | $0.00695 | Free to CDN partners; 3x stored free |
| Bunny Storage | No | No | No | Unknown (async replication) | 500 RPS per zone | $1 monthly minimum | None | $0 / $0 | $0.01 | Free to Bunny CDN |
| Hetzner Object Storage | Not supported on versioned buckets; unversioned is undocumented | Same | Same | Undocumented | 750 requests per second per bucket | **64 kB minimum billable object** | Yes | $0 / $0 | about $0.0089 | 1 TB included |
| Wasabi | Unknown | Unknown | Unknown | Undocumented | Undocumented | 1 TB minimum bill, 90-day retention, 4 KB minimum | Yes | $0 / $0 | $0.0078 | Free up to stored volume |
| DigitalOcean Spaces | No | No | No | Undocumented | 800 operations per second per bucket | 4 KiB minimum | API only | $0 / $0 | $0.02 | 1,024 GiB included |

**Implications:**
- **Only S3, GCS, Azure, Tigris and Scaleway document what the invariants need.** R2 documents the headers but not their behavior under concurrent writers, so it needs a spike before anyone relies on it. B2, Bunny, Hetzner, Wasabi and DO Spaces should not hold anything that needs a conditional write.
- **R2 has no conditional delete and no versioning.** A single-use magic link on R2 is consumed by a conditional PUT that writes `used_at`, and the second consumer gets a 412. An accidental overwrite or a moderation bug is unrecoverable unless the app writes its own backups.
- **R2's and GCS's one-write-per-second-per-key limit** means shared aggregates (a group roster, a RSVP counter, a feed index) top out at about one successful write a second, while one-file-per-item designs never hit it.
- **None of these can expire an object at a precise time.** Lifecycle rules run in days (R2 "within 24 hours"), so 15-minute magic links and sessions must check `expires_at` in the file body.
- **Nobody offers multi-key atomicity** ("There is no way to make atomic updates across keys", S3 docs).
- **Small-object traps:** Hetzner's 64 kB minimum makes a 0.5 KB file about 128 times more expensive to store, and a 50-million-object bucket cap is crossed within a year at 10,000 groups mid. Wasabi charges for deleted and overwritten bytes for 90 days, so session and token churn saves nothing.
- **CDN caches JSON.** An overwritten object keeps being served until the cache TTL expires or you purge, and 404s are cached, so browser-fetched JSON needs explicit `Cache-Control` and purge on write. R2's `r2.dev` domain is for development only, and R2 custom domains are capped at 100 per bucket, so 1,000 paid groups can't each use one without a Worker or several buckets.

## Which invariants files can enforce

On a store with documented conditional writes, most invariants become a key design plus a conditional PUT.

| Requirement | Files alone? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Unique RSVP per meeting and email | **Yes** | Key `rsvp/{meeting}/{HMAC(email)}`, written with create-if-absent. Use HMAC with a server secret, not a bare SHA-256, since a bare hash can be brute-forced from a LIST |
| Unique group slug and custom domain | **Yes** | Same: `slugs/{slug}`, `domains/{host}` |
| Exactly one owner | **Yes** | Owner only in `group.json`, changed by one conditional PUT |
| 150-member cap | **Yes, with limits** | One `members.json` roster per group updated by compare-and-swap. Serializes joins (R2 allows 1 write per second per key), and the roster grows without bound on paid groups |
| Single-use magic link, consumed on button press | **Yes** | Conditional delete on S3, or a conditional PUT marking it used on R2. Expiry is checked in the body |
| Sessions | **Yes** | Stateless signed cookie plus a per-user `valid_after` object |
| 3 custom messages per group per day | **Yes** | Up to 3 slot keys per group per day |
| Storage quota per group | **Yes, with limits** | A compare-and-swap ledger file, or a serialized writer |
| Reminder sent exactly once | **As a claim only** | Create-if-absent marker `sent/{meeting}/{start}`. Files can't supply the timer, retries or exactly-once email delivery |
| Rate limits (3 magic links per 15 min, 10 RSVPs per IP per minute) | **Partial** | Fixed-window slot keys only, and every attacker request becomes a billed write. Do this in process memory, at the CDN or WAF, or in a small store |
| Multi-key changes (ownership transfer, join with cap and preferences, delete a meeting only if it has no RSVPs, account deletion cascade) | **No** | Needs one aggregate document per unit, a journal, or a database |
| Organizer analytics, "all RSVPs for this email", most active members | **No** | Maintained rollups or reverse indexes, or a batch into SQLite or DuckDB |
| Erasure of one person's data | **No** | Needs reverse indexes and a resumable sweeper, plus purging rendered pages, CDN, search bundles, old versions, replicas and backups |
| Point-in-time recovery | **No** | Bucket versioning (S3, B2), snapshots, or an app-level journal. R2 has none |

**What a coordinator costs** (at 10,000 groups): SQLite inside the Go process $0; Cloudflare Durable Objects or D1 about $5 (the Workers Paid floor); DynamoDB about $1 to $1.30; Upstash Redis about $6; Turso free tier $0 through 10,000 groups; Neon about $10 to $19. The choice is about correctness and operations, not cost.

**Private data must live in a separate private bucket** behind the app: emails, RSVP lists, virtual meeting links and tokens can never sit in the CDN-served tree, because public JSON is public and a misconfiguration would leak every address.

## Granularity and request costs

Which file holds what matters more than which store. Five granularities were compared on R2 (persist plus render inputs, per million messages, low / mid / high activity):

| Design | Per million messages, stateless renderer | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **S1: one file per post** | $50 to $78 | Every render lists and reads the siblings. The user's proposal |
| **S2: one file per thread**, compare-and-swap | $11 to $13 | One GET to render. Bounded rewrite size (a thread is 2 to 30 messages, about 15 KB) |
| S3a: append-only JSONL per group per day | $11.5 to $16.5 | |
| S3b: JSONL per group per month | $4.9 to $5.7 | Cheapest on R2 (bytes are free) but rewrites about 750 KB per message and moves 5.6 TB written and 7.9 TB read per month at 10,000 groups high. Better as a sealed archive format |
| S4: per-post files plus a per-thread manifest | $15.7 to $22.7 | About 1.4x per-thread, with an orphan-post window between the two writes |
| Any of them with a **warm renderer** (state and ETags cached in the single writer) | About $9 (2 PUTs) | The granularity debate is mostly moot if the writer keeps a cache |

Monthly request totals on R2 **including one page PUT per message** (the PRD's purge matrix regenerates one page per event), verified by the second agent:

| Groups | S1 per-post, stateless (low / mid / high) | S2 per-thread (low / mid / high) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | $0.05 / $3.17 / $10.77 | $0.02 / $0.77 / $2.59 |
| 1,000 | $0.55 / $31.75 / $107.72 | about $0.2 / $7.7 / $25.9 |
| 10,000 | $5.48 / $317 / $1,077 | about $2 / $77 / $259 |

With a **warm renderer** and 1 page PUT per message, every granularity is about $0.90 / $40 / $135 at 10,000 groups. Without any coalescing, group-page regeneration is the biggest lever: coalescing 5 regenerations into 1 cuts S2's 10,000-group high total from $259 to about $110.

**The estimates disagreed.** The seven topics priced "one file per post, stateless" for 10,000 groups high at $181, $359, $383 and $1,305, a 3.6 to 3.9 times spread. The differences are how many pages each event regenerates (0, 1 or 2), how many siblings the renderer reads per message, and whether the renderer keeps state. This has to be settled with a **measured operations-per-event table from a prototype**, not more modeling. The lower figures are the better-supported ones (they follow the PRD's purge matrix), and the $1,305 figure is an upper bound.

**Other price points:**
- Tigris (per-request pricing, per-post files): $1.13 at 100 groups mid, $11.80 at 1,000, $118.54 at 10,000 (high: $383.54).
- Zero-request stores at 10,000 groups high, month 12: Scaleway about $77 (storage $50.90 plus renderer egress $26.49), B2 about $19. Both cost 3 to 19 times less than R2 request pricing, but B2 has no conditional writes and Scaleway has an unpublished PUT rate limit.
- **Read path is unpriced.** None of the topics priced per-view reads. If feed and comment fragments are fetched per view (the PRD serves them as uncached fragments), a per-post layout could cost about $102 a month in LIST and GET requests at 10,000 groups (using a placeholder of 400,000 members loading the feed 4 times a month), against $13 for per-thread files. An in-process cache would remove most of it.
- **A real design decision hides here:** whether posts are baked into the static page (write-amplified: 0, 1 or 2 page PUTs cost $0, $67.50 or $135 a month at 10,000 groups high) or fetched per view (read-amplified). It is the single largest cost swing and was never settled.

**Attachments dominate bytes** (96 to 97%) and are identical in every design. At 1,000 groups mid they reach about 82 GB after 12 months (about $1 a month on R2). At 10,000 groups high they reach 2.7 TB after 12 months ($40.50 a month on R2) and 8.1 TB after 36 months ($121.50 on R2, $56.30 on B2). Storage growth, private access via signed URLs, orphan uploads and quota decrement on delete are the same across designs and need their own owner in the model.

## Queries, jobs, analytics and lifecycle on files

Everything in scope can be built without SQL, and request dollars are small. The problem is object count, index rebuilds and recovery.

- **Reminders job (every 5 minutes):** keep a date-partitioned index `due/{yyyymmddThhmm}/{meeting}`, LIST it on a schedule, and claim each reminder with a create-if-absent marker. About $0.04 a month for the scan and $0.07 to $0.53 for the audience at 10,000 groups. A naive scan of all meeting keys would cost $14 a month.
- **Organizer analytics:** compute on demand with a one-hour cache from LIST-only derivations, about $0.0001 to $0.0003 per view. At 4 views per group per month that is $5 to $11 a month at 10,000 groups. Recomputing nightly for everyone costs $38 to $85. The PRD's "member growth" counts current members only, so no history is needed.
- **Directory:** one compressed JSON file (40 to 50 KB) up to about 1,000 groups, geohash-3 tiles above that, with the browser doing the sorting. Rewriting every tile daily costs under $0.06 a month. This changes the PRD's server-rendered, distance-sorted directory, and needs a design decision.
- **Search:** the PRD needs directory search only, not in-group post search. Static-index search of post threads (Pagefind) was priced at $11 to $325 a month at 10,000 groups and is **not required**.
- **Deletion:** files can't find one person's files without reverse indexes, and deleting a file doesn't remove it from rendered pages, CDN caches, search bundles, old versions, replicas or backups. It needs an erasure runbook and a short, documented backup retention window.
- **Backups:** at the high case with per-post files, 10,000 groups reach about 562 million objects after 36 months. A full restore then costs about $2,500 in PUTs and takes anywhere from 9 hours (17,000 objects per second) to 156 hours (1,000 per second). **Do not use AWS Backup** on small objects: it bills each as 128 KiB, about $1,300 a month at 12 months and $3,800 at 36 months. Use an append-only journal plus daily packed snapshots to a second provider.
- **Point-in-time recovery:** R2 has no versioning or replication. SQLite with Litestream (about 1 to 10 seconds of loss) or Postgres WAL gives real PITR, and D1 has 30-day minute-level restore.

## The local-disk variant (single VPS)

Files on a VPS filesystem are fast enough (1,065 durable file creates per second measured, against a worst case of about 58 posts per second), and POSIX gives atomic create (`O_EXCL`), atomic replace (write, fsync, rename) and locks for free with no request fees. But:

- **Inodes run out.** Default ext4 makes one inode per 16 KiB, so a 40 GiB disk holds 2.6 million files. At 1,000 groups mid that is exhausted in about 5.6 months, at 1,000 groups high in about 1.7 months, and at 10,000 groups low in about 8.5 months.
- **Space:** a 0.5 KB file costs about 4.2 to 4.7 KB on default ext4, XFS and ZFS (8 to 9 times), against about 555 bytes per SQLite row. Formatting ext4 with 1 KiB inodes and inline data cuts this to about 1.1 KB (measured only offline). Tunable, not a hard limit.
- **Files after 36 months:** 100 groups mid 1.7M, 1,000 groups mid 17M, 10,000 groups mid 170M, 10,000 groups high 548M.
- **Backup and restore:** on warm storage 17M files take about 5 to 28 minutes to copy or restore, and hour-scale times begin at 55M+ files or slow storage. A live tree copied with rsync or tar gives a torn, inconsistent snapshot, so use filesystem snapshots (ZFS or btrfs) with incremental send, provider snapshots plus an offsite copy, or Litestream for SQLite.
- **Missing pieces:** no compare-and-swap on file content (rename replaces unconditionally), no counters or rate limits, no expiry, no secondary indexes, no seconds-level continuous replication for a file tree, no data checksums on ext4 or XFS, and schema changes mean rewriting millions of files.
- **Guidance:** do not make one file per post the system of record beyond about 1 to 3 million files. If canonical files are wanted, pack per conversation (one JSON document per thread) behind a per-group lock, which cuts file count about 16 times and disk about 5.8 times at the mid case, and keep uniqueness, indexes, counters and tokens in SQLite or memory.

## Prior art

- **Works for files:** derived, read-mostly artifacts on a CDN (Cargo sparse index, CocoaPods CDN, Homebrew JSON API), and low-write, few-writer content (Grav, Kirby, roughly 10,000 to 60,000 pages).
- **Object-storage databases batch:** SlateDB, turbopuffer, WarpStream, Neon, Iceberg, Delta and DuckLake all pack many records per object and add a small strongly-consistent commit point (a manifest compare-and-swap, a WAL cursor, a metadata store or a SQL catalog). None writes one object per record.
- **Per-comment-file systems shrank or died:** Staticman's shared instance hit GitHub's 5,000-requests-per-hour quota, and the comment engines that lasted (Isso, Remark42) chose SQLite or BoltDB. Decap CMS is a weaker witness than first stated: one reporter's quota problem was traced to browser caching failing on their network.
- **Don't use for state:** Workers KV and Tigris global buckets (eventually consistent) for RSVP state or uniqueness, and git or GitHub APIs as the write path.

## Recommendation

1. **Design 1 as the default:** SQLite (or Postgres) as the system of record with Litestream, and files as derived pages, JSON projections and directory tiles served from the CDN, regenerated by a debounced in-process job (coalesce 5 to 30 seconds per page key, never faster than one write per second per key). Attachments in R2 or B2.
2. **Use Design 2 only if posts must be durable public JSON off the VPS.** Then one immutable JSON object per post plus a SQLite index, nightly compaction into per-group-month segments, and the coordinator for the invariants. Put the files on Scaleway or B2 if request fees matter and R2 only after the conditional-write spike passes.
3. **Treat Design 3 (no database) as a bound, not a plan.** It leaves holes for exact rate limits, sub-day expiry, multi-key operations, analytics and point-in-time recovery.
4. **Settle two design questions first,** because they swing cost more than any storage choice: whether posts are baked into pages or fetched per view, and how many pages each event regenerates.
5. **For the cost model:** price the database-first baseline (Litestream at 1 s, 10 s and 60 s), the D1 or Durable Objects serverless baseline, the hybrid on R2 and on a zero-request store, and the naive per-post bound. Keep attachments and backups as fixed lines, and price with and without free allowances.

## Spikes before committing

1. R2 conditional PUT correctness: 2 and 30 concurrent `If-None-Match: *` and `If-Match` writers to one key, over 1,000 rounds, expecting exactly one winner. Observe 412, 409 and 429 rates and whether failed conditionals are billed.
2. The same suite on Scaleway and Tigris, and re-test B2, Bunny and Hetzner: is the answer a 412, a 501, or a silent overwrite?
3. Measure operations per event (RSVP, login, message, join, reminder tick, page regeneration) with a counter on a prototype, to replace the modeled counts.
4. Litestream on the target VPS: real PUTs per month at 1 s, 10 s and 60 s, and restore time for 10 GB and 100 GB.
5. Cold-cache file-tree behavior on ext4 or XFS on NVMe: create, tar, rsync and restore 1M and 10M files, and inode use.

## Open questions

1. **Availability target.** Is a single VPS writer with minutes of restore acceptable, or is a second writer required? A second writer pushes the coordinator toward D1, Durable Objects or Postgres.
2. **Compute host.** The PRD's Go process on a VPS uses the S3 API, but several cost tricks need Cloudflare Workers bindings. If everything moved to Workers plus D1, the VPS and Postgres disappear and files add nothing, and nobody costed that comparator.
3. **Erasure policy.** The PRD keeps anonymous RSVPs with the email after account deletion. The deletion brief says to remove personal data but keep counts. The key design depends on which.
4. **Where the pre-rendered HTML lives:** a bucket, or the origin's disk with a CDN pull (the PRD's Bunny pull zone implies the latter). Bunny's 500-hostname cap and its purge limits are unchecked for per-hostname purging at 1,000 paid groups.
5. **Not covered by any topic:** Stripe subscription lifecycle (webhook idempotency, the rule that a lapsed paid group keeps existing members but blocks joins over 150), platform-admin listing and suspension, email bounce suppression and outbox retry (the River replacement), retention and archival, and the cost of a schema migration.

## Data quality notes

- **Not comparable across topics:** free-tier treatment (some topics apply R2's 10 GB and 1M-request free allowances, others don't), horizons (month 12 vs 36), and message counts (440 vs 448 for the mid case).
- **R2's billing rounds up to the next million requests**, so sub-$5 figures can differ from a literal invoice by up to $4.86 a month.
- **Suspicious inputs:** the coordinator unit counts (170 write units, 289 Redis commands and 320 rows per group per month) are estimates, the 10 times peak factor is an assumption, and the "$48 to $96 Postgres host" at 10,000 groups is unsized.
- **All timings were measured on ZFS inside a container with a warm cache**, and should be re-measured on the target VPS.
